## What I worked on

Task 7 turned the eight fixtures into a searchable Browse screen. People could
filter locally by brand, product name, or SKU, see product cards, reach empty,
loading, and error states, and open a placeholder Product Detail route.

## Problem discovered

The first product list needed more than a successful render. Without reachable
non-happy paths, error and empty-state components could exist in source while
remaining impossible to evaluate. Filter and Sort also appeared in the design
before their real behavior was defined.

## Decision made

Browse implemented useful local search and navigation, kept Filter and Sort as
disabled placeholders, and added `__error__` as a deterministic mock trigger.
The screen also included an end-of-list placeholder required by the recorded
flow review.

## Evidence captured

The shared Tasks 6–7 commit contains the product list, search, card navigation,
and state handling. A focused follow-up commit makes the error state directly
reachable. These commits show implementation; Task 10 later supplied the
broader UX evidence.

## Result

Browse became the first complete catalog surface without requiring backend
credentials or network availability. Its deliberate limitations made the next
screen and later connection work easier to isolate.
