## What I worked on

Task 8 replaced the placeholder route with a composed Product Detail screen.
It added product metadata, Eazy Score, Community Score and count, offers by
size, a rating breakdown, session-local My Rating context, description, and a
working route to the upcoming rating form.

## Problem discovered

The summary product type was not a safe owner for every detail concern.
Commerce offers, community aggregates, and user-specific rating state have
different lifecycles. Flattening them into one product object would make the
later connected client harder to reason about.

## Decision made

The task introduced a composed detail contract and kept My Rating separate
from public catalog data. Price display derived from usable offers, score copy
handled missing values, and unknown identifiers produced an explicit not-found
state. The screen was delivered in sequential packets rather than one large
rewrite.

## Evidence captured

The data-contract commit fixes the ownership boundary before UI composition.
Pull request #9 and its merge commit record the completed route, fixtures,
formatting utility, shared rating row, and contract documentation.

## Result

Product Detail became a useful destination with enough honest sparse states to
support UX review. It still depended on mock data and did not claim durable
ratings or real offers.
